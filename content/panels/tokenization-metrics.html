04 · Tokenization — Metrics

Reading the Instruments

The discretization gate emits a per-feature table and a four-panel figure. This panel is the permanent reading guide: what each metric measures, its range, and what counts as good / not-enough-information / bad / informative — with no specific run's numbers, so it stays true across phases. The headline question it all serves: does turning a number into a token throw away what the number told us about the physics? — and, ultimately, how close can we get to the detector's channel capacity.

GOOD — premise holds NOT ENOUGH INFO — stats / tune BAD — premise fails INFORMATIVE — design signal, not pass/fail

What we are ultimately after: capacity

A detector is a noisy channel carrying the truth-level event $x$ to the reconstructed event $y$. Its capacity $C$ is the maximum mutual information any analysis could extract — the ceiling on achievable signal/background separation. Everything the gate measures is a mutual-information quantity that lives below that ceiling, in one bracket:

$I_\text{NCE} \;\le\; I_\text{empirical}(x;y) \;\le\; C \;\le\; C_\text{Gauss}$

The gate's per-feature MI is a marginal building block — the information in one feature about the label — not $C$ itself ($C$ is the joint maximum, estimated later from the response matrix). What the gate actually tests is the data-processing inequality: binning is a processing step, so $I(\text{bin}(x);y) \le I(x;y)$. If binning preserves the MI, tokenization sits as a lossless step inside that bracket. MI retention is the DPI, measured.

Metric reference — separation & information

mi_retainmi_bin / mi_raw — fraction of information surviving tokenization (the DPI, measured) ~0 – ~1 (>1 = noise) >0.90 0.75–0.90 <0.75
Why MI magnitude is not a pass/fail. Under heavy class imbalance the label entropy $H(Y)$ is tiny, so absolute MI is small even for a strong discriminator. The verdict therefore reads separation from AUC (rank-based, imbalance-robust) and reads preservation from the retention ratio (where the imbalance cancels). A retention slightly above 1.0 is estimator residual (Miller–Madow on finite samples), read as ≈1.0.

Metric reference — occupancy (informative, not pass/fail)

These two do not pass or fail the gate. They are informative — they tell you which features tokenize cleanly and which need special handling, feeding the tokenizer design directly.

The verdict, and the summary fields

Per lane the gate resolves one of three states, judged the imbalance-robust way:

VerdictConditionMeaning
PASSmedian retention > 0.90 and median ΔAUC > −0.02 on separating featuresbinning preserves ranking and information — tokenization safe on this lane
WATCHseparation exists but retention or ΔAUC erodes ittune N_BINS / strategy before trusting it
FLOOREDbest AUC < 0.55 (no feature separates)nothing to preserve here — trigger an earlier-snapshot sample

The cross-lane phase1_summary.csv carries one row per lane: n_sig / n_bkg (class counts), best_AUC, sep_feats (features above 0.55), med_dAUC, med_MI_retain, collapsed_feats, low_stat (smaller class < 200 → verdict indicative only), and verdict.

The four graph panels

ΔAUC bars (top features)

Ranking lost to binning, per top feature. Good: bars hug zero. Watch: a bar past −0.02 (orange).

MI raw vs binned scatter

Each feature a point; the diagonal is perfect retention. Good: points on the line. Watch: a point dropping below — that feature lost information (often the most MI-rich one, which has the most to lose).

Vocabulary-size knee

Mean retention vs N_BINS. Rises and plateaus; the knee is the smallest vocabulary that keeps the information — it sizes the embedding vocabulary. A late knee means 16 bins is too few.

Bin-occupancy heatmap

Feature × bin fraction. Black cells = empty bins. A row that is one bright cell = a collapsed feature. Informative: this is the tokenizer to-do list, made visual.

What we have once we establish the channel's capacity

The gate confirms tokenization is lossless; capacity is the larger prize it serves. Once $C$ is estimated (truth→reco response matrix → Blahut–Arimoto), three things become available that per-feature MI alone cannot give:

  • A performance ceiling. $C$ is the most separation any classifier could achieve on this data. Comparing a model's achieved MI to $C$ says how much is left on the table — whether to build a better model or whether the detector simply cannot do more.
  • The capacity-achieving prior. Blahut–Arimoto also returns the input distribution that saturates $C$ — which truth configurations carry the most recoverable information.
  • The epistemic-floor check. The per-stage entropy floor $H(p_k)$ versus a model's predictive entropy tests that the model never claims to know more than the physics allows.
Where these metrics sit. The discretization metrics fill in only the left of the bracket — the measured, marginal MI. Capacity ($C$, $C_\text{Gauss}$) is the right side, computed from the response matrix and independent of any embedding. The InfoNCE term $I_\text{NCE}$ arrives later, from Evt2Vec training. The bracket is the map; this gate is one rung of it.
